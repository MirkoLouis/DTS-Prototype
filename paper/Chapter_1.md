# **The Problem and Its Background**

## **1.1 Introduction**

In the modern landscape of public administration, Document Tracking Systems (DTS) serve as the essential digital infrastructure for monitoring the lifecycle of official correspondence. A DTS is a specialized information system designed to record the intake, movement, and final disposition of physical and digital files within an organization. For large-scale entities like the Department of Education (DepEd) Division of Iligan City, these systems are no longer optional tools; they are foundational to ensuring that personnel records, financial requests, and academic reports are processed in an orderly and transparent manner. By providing a centralized view of where a document is and who is currently handling it, these systems transform abstract administrative processes into visible, manageable workflows (Frankel et al., 2018).

Before the introduction of digital tracking, government offices relied almost exclusively on manual logbooks and physical routing slips. In this traditional setup, a document’s journey was recorded by hand at every desk it reached, often requiring staff to flip through thick paper ledgers to find a specific entry. While this method was sufficient for lower volumes of work, it was highly susceptible to human error, physical damage, and loss (Bala, 2020; Piloton, 2023). There was no way to search for a document instantly, and the lack of a centralized backup meant that if a logbook was lost, the entire history of those documents disappeared with it. The transition to digital DTS models was driven by the need for speed, searchability, and the ability to manage thousands of records without the physical limitations of paper.

The primary goal of a Document Tracking System is to eliminate the uncertainty and lack of accountability often found in manual workflows. Without a tracking system, documents frequently enter a "black hole" where their status and location are unknown to both the submitter and the management. This leads to systemic delays, misplaced files, and a phenomenon known as "Ghost Documents," which are requests that move through the office without any official record (Rokosu, 2025). DTSs aim to solve these issues by providing a real-time audit trail, ensuring that every hand-off is timestamped and every delay is identifiable. This digital accountability is particularly critical under laws like Republic Act No. 11032, which mandates that government actions be time-bound and verifiable to prevent bureaucratic slow-downs (Republic Act No. 11032, 2018).

This thesis project differs from most standard Document Tracking Systems by moving beyond simple database logging into the realm of "Mathematical-Based Security." While traditional systems rely on the hope that administrators will not change data (Policy-Based Security), this project implements a "Trust Builder" architecture using SHA-256 hash-chaining and Ed25519 digital signatures. Every log entry is mathematically linked to the one before it, making it impossible to secretly alter the history of a document. Furthermore, by requiring a Security PIN to generate cryptographic signatures for critical actions, the system ensures non-repudiation—meaning a user cannot later deny that they performed a specific action. Most common systems lack this level of cryptographic protection, making them vulnerable to "silent tampering" that this project is designed to prevent (Bajoria, 2025; Crosby & Wallach, 2009).

For IT students, this study is a vital bridge between theoretical classroom concepts and the practical demands of the software industry. Developing this system allows the researchers to apply advanced topics—such as cryptographic salt derivation, database indexing for millions of records, and asynchronous background processing—to solve a tangible community problem. Beyond technical skills, the project teaches the importance of "Security by Design" and the ethical responsibility of a developer to protect the integrity of government data. By building a system that directly impacts the efficiency of the DepEd Division Office, the researchers gain a deeper understanding of how IT can be used as a tool for governance, transparency, and the reduction of corruption in the public sector.

## **1.2 Background of the Study**

The origin of this study began with an empirical observation visit to the DepEd Division Office of Iligan City in December 2025, facilitated by the official responsible for the researchers' survey authorization. During this visit, researchers witnessed a "Digital Traffic Jam" at the guest intake kiosks. Upon reporting these observations to the IT and Records Departments, the researchers identified a critical technical failure: the existing DTS lacked robust queue management, causing concurrent submission attempts to result in a race condition (FormKiQ, 2023; SunSmart Global, 2022). This failure meant that one guest's print job would often overwrite another’s, leaving citizens with incorrect tracking information and causing significant administrative backlog.

Beyond the visible failure at the kiosks, deeper consultation with the Records and IT Departments revealed that the existing DTS was not being utilized to its full potential. Specifically, a large volume of internal documents frequently bypassed the initial digital submission process, resulting in a systemic lack of "Tracking Codes"—the primary keys essential for the system’s database integrity. To manage these unrecorded documents and ensure operational continuity, the Records Department established a "Shadow System" (Behrens, 2009; Rentrop & Zimmermann, 2020) using Google Forms and Google Sheets. Staff would manually transcribe document metadata—such as client names and purposes—from printed forms into Google Sheets to maintain a searchable record that the main DTS could not provide.

This fragmentation extends into the releasing phase, where another shadow system is employed. Because internal documents often lack an intake tracking code, the releasing department generates independent "Releasing Codes" for formality and documentation. This practice, while providing a temporary solution for the staff, results in a disconnected and technically flawed database structure where the lifecycle of a document cannot be reliably traced from intake to release. The reliance on unencrypted Google Sheets for over 10,000 entries (Empirical Observation, 2025) represents a significant risk to data integrity, as these files lack the ACID compliance and mathematical security needed to prevent unauthorized or accidental modifications (Haag & Eckhardt, 2024).

When comparing this situation to other standard Document Tracking Systems used in the public sector, a clear gap in functionality emerges. Most existing DTS solutions are designed to handle either only external guest intakes or only internal staff workflows, but rarely both under a unified tracking architecture. In the case of DepEd Iligan, previous digital attempts failed because they did not account for the high volume of internal correspondence that bypasses the formal intake process. While other systems may have digitized the "hand-over" process, they failed to provide the mathematical assurance that those logs remained untampered over time. The institution was essentially relying on "Policy-Based Security"—the hope that staff would follow the rules—rather than "Mathematical-Based Security" that makes rules impossible to break (Bajoria, 2025).

Following these revelations and the recommendation of the IT Head, the researchers recognized that a standard, "off-the-shelf" tracking system would not solve the problem. The study therefore pivoted to create a custom, high-security prototype tailored to the unique workflow of DepEd Iligan City. This solution is specifically designed to meet the client’s strict constraints: it must be built using PHP and restricted to a local, on-premise network (LAN) to ensure data sovereignty. By integrating "Trust Builder" hash-chaining and Ed25519 signatures, the proposed system is not just another tracking tool; it is a specialized security artifact designed to restore institutional trust and provide a permanent solution to the proliferation of informal, insecure tracking methods.

## **1.3 Statement of the Problem**

The primary challenge facing the DepEd Division Office of Iligan City is the technical instability and incomplete operational integration of its existing Document Tracking System (DTS), which results in data corruption and the proliferation of insecure administrative practices. A critical manifestation of this instability is the occurrence of race conditions within the guest intake kiosks, where concurrent submissions often result in simultaneous print jobs overwriting one another (FormKiQ, 2023; SunSmart Global, 2022). This technical flaw leads to the immediate loss of tracking data for citizens, creating a "Digital Traffic Jam" that undermines institutional reliability and prevents the system from maintaining a consistent record of external requests.

Beyond these visible failures, the organization suffers from a systemic fragmentation of its tracking architecture due to the operational bypassing of internal correspondence. A significant volume of documents frequently skips the formal digital intake process, resulting in a database that lacks the mandatory tracking codes—the primary keys—essential for end-to-end traceability. To compensate for these gaps and ensure operational continuity, the Records Department has resorted to the use of "Shadow Systems" (Behrens, 2009; Rentrop & Zimmermann, 2020) using unencrypted platforms like Google Forms and Google Sheets. While these informal methods provide a temporary solution for staff, they introduce severe data integrity risks and lack the mathematical security and ACID compliance necessary to protect sensitive government records (Haag & Eckhardt, 2024).

This lack of a unified tracking protocol further destabilizes the final stages of the document lifecycle. Because internal documents often lack an intake record, the releasing department is forced to generate independent "releasing codes" for the sake of formality. This practice creates a disconnected and technically flawed database structure where a document's journey cannot be reliably traced from intake to final release (Empirical Observation, 2025). Consequently, the institution is unable to provide a verifiable audit trail, leading to the proliferation of "Ghost Documents" (Rokosu, 2025) and failing to meet the accountability and transparency mandates of the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act (Republic Act No. 11032, 2018).

## **1.4 Objectives of the Study**

**General Objective:**
To design, develop, and evaluate a high-security Document Tracking System (DTS) for DepEd Iligan City that enforces a mandatory intake protocol to eliminate unofficial "ghost documents," while integrating advanced cryptographic measures—specifically SHA-256 hash-chaining and Ed25519 digital signatures—to ensure absolute data integrity and non-repudiation.

**Specific Objectives:**
1.  **Enforce Mandatory Intake Protocols:** Develop a technical gatekeeping mechanism that prevents any document record from being processed without a verified, system-generated tracking code, ensuring that internal documents cannot bypass the formal intake procedure.
2.  **Develop a Secure Cryptographic Audit Trail:** Implement a SHA-256 hash-chaining mechanism within the "Trust Builder" module to mathematically link each log entry to its predecessor, creating a tamper-evident history for every document.
3.  **Implement Digital Signatures for Non-Repudiation:** Integrate an Ed25519-based signing interface (Bernstein et al., 2012) that requires users to initialize a cryptographic keypair via a Security PIN, providing mathematical proof of identity for every receipt and release action.
4.  **Synchronize Physical and Digital Workflows:** Utilize QR code technology to facilitate rapid scanning and verification during department-to-department transfers, ensuring the digital status accurately reflects the physical location of the file.
5.  **Evaluate Technical Integrity and Usability:** Conduct a comprehensive evaluation to measure the prototype’s ability to detect unauthorized modifications and assess its operational efficiency in a high-volume administrative environment.

## **1.5 Significance of the Study**

The findings and technical deliverables of this study will provide substantial operational and security benefits to the DepEd Division of Iligan City and the broader academic community. Specifically, the following groups will directly benefit from the implementation of this system:

*   **Records Department Personnel:** The system eliminates the tedious and error-prone reliance on manual "shadow systems" (Google Forms and Sheets). By enforcing a mandatory intake protocol and providing an automated tracking architecture, it significantly reduces manual transcription and administrative fatigue, allowing staff to process documents with greater speed and accuracy.
*   **DepEd IT Department:** The prototype provides the IT staff with a highly secure, PHP-based local solution that strictly adheres to the division's on-premise network (LAN) constraints. By replacing unencrypted shadow systems with a centralized database secured by SHA-256 hash-chaining, it drastically reduces the organization's vulnerability to insider threats and data corruption.
*   **DepEd Management and Division Officials:** The integration of Ed25519 digital signatures and QR code scanning provides management with mathematical proof of action (non-repudiation) and verifiable timestamps. This ensures institutional transparency and strict compliance with the accountability mandates of the Ease of Doing Business Act (Republic Act No. 11032).
*   **Guests and the General Public:** Citizens submitting requests will experience a more reliable and professional digital service. The elimination of kiosk race conditions guarantees that their document data will not be overwritten, preventing frustration and ensuring real-time visibility into the status of their requests.
*   **Future IT Researchers:** This study serves as a practical, documented reference for implementing advanced cryptographic features—specifically blockchain-inspired hash-chaining and Ed25519 signatures—within resource-constrained, centralized relational databases (RDBMS) in the public sector.

## **1.6 Scope and Delimitation**

**Scope:** 
The study is geographically and operationally bounded within the DepEd Division Office of Iligan City. It covers the end-to-end document request lifecycle, beginning from the initial guest intake at the kiosks, through internal department-to-department transfers, and concluding with the final physical release of the document. The system serves as a digital synchronization layer and an immutable logbook, utilizing the `DocumentLog` architecture to record and verify the physical location and state of documents in real-time.

**Delimitations:** 
The project is strictly limited to a local area network (LAN) deployment as mandated by the institutional IT department to ensure data sovereignty. Technical implementation is restricted to the PHP programming language; specifically, the prototype is built using the Laravel 12 framework to provide a modern, optimized structure while adhering to the client's language constraints. Furthermore, the system is designed to digitize the tracking metadata and the security audit trail rather than replacing the physical storage or movement of original paper documents. It does not extend to the DepEd Regional Office or other external divisions.

## **1.7 Definition of Terms**

For the purpose of this study, the following terms are defined to ensure a clear understanding of the technical concepts presented:

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **ACID Compliance** | A set of database properties (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability) that guarantee that transactions are processed reliably, ensuring data integrity even in the event of system failures. |
| **Active Guard** | A specialized security middleware that verifies a document's current database state against its last signed record before authorizing any new action. |
| **Auto-Freeze** | An automated defensive mechanism that locks a document’s record from further processing if the Active Guard detects unauthorized data manipulation. |
| **Ed25519** | A high-performance, elliptic curve digital signature scheme used to provide cryptographic proof of identity for administrative actions. |
| **Hash-Chaining** | A cryptographic method where the hash of a previous log entry is included in the current entry, creating a tamper-evident audit trail where the history cannot be altered without breaking the chain. |
| **Micro-sharding** | An architectural pattern that isolates the security hash-chains into document-specific segments, ensuring the system remains scalable and fast as the record volume grows. |
| **Non-Repudiation** | A security assurance that provides proof of the origin and integrity of data, making it impossible for a user to deny having performed a specific action or signature. |
| **Race Condition** | A technical failure occurring when multiple processes attempt to access or modify shared data simultaneously, resulting in unintended behavior or data corruption. |
| **SHA-256** | A secure cryptographic hash function that generates a fixed-size, unique digital fingerprint of data, utilized in this study for constructing the hash-chain. |
| **Shadow System** | Unofficial IT solutions, such as manual Google Sheets or Forms, deployed by staff to bypass or supplement failing official systems, often leading to data fragmentation and security risks. |
| **Tracking Code** | A unique, system-generated alphanumeric identifier that serves as the primary key for a document, enabling its end-to-end traceability across the organization. |
| **Trust Builder** | The centralized software module responsible for calculating hashes, managing chains, and maintaining the mathematical integrity of the Document Tracking System. |
