## **3.1 Research Design**

This study will employ the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology, an
approach suited for developing and evaluating innovative information
technology artifacts that address identified organizational or societal
problems (Hevner et al., 2004). DSR focuses on both the creation of
purposeful artifacts and the generation of design knowledge that
contributes to theory and practice (March & Smith, 1995). ...... \<cite
current studies that uses DSRM\>

The study will adopt the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM)
proposed by Peffers et al. (2007), which provides a systematic process
for developing, demonstrating, and evaluating artifacts in Information
Systems research. The six key stages that will guide the research are:

1.  Problem identification and motivation

2.  Definition of the objectives of a solution

3.  Design and development of the artifact

4.  Demonstration of the artifact

5.  Evaluation of the artifact

6.  Communication of the results

These stages will be iteratively executed following Hevner's (2007)
three-cycle model---the Relevance Cycle (connection to the real-world
environment), the Design Cycle (building and evaluating the artifact),
and the Rigor Cycle (linking to the existing scientific knowledge base).

## **3.3 Problem Identification and Motivation**

The study will begin with an in-depth analysis of the problem
environment to identify current isasues, inefficiencies, or unmet needs
in the target domain (e.g., education, health, or digital service
delivery). A combination of literature review, needs assessment, and
expert consultation will be conducted to define the practical problem
and its significance.

The problem statement will emphasize:

-   The gap between current system capabilities and desired performance.

-   The impact of the identified problem on users or organizations.

-   The motivation for creating an innovative IT-based solution.

The output of this stage will be a clear articulation of the problem and
its relevance both practically and theoretically.

## **3.6 Artifact Design and Development Plan**

The artifact, \[name of the system/framework/etc\], will be developed
through an iterative prototyping approach. Each cycle will include:

1.  Requirement analysis and conceptual design

2.  Prototype development

3.  Expert or user evaluation

4.  Refinement and re-design

Documentation will be maintained through a design log containing records
of design decisions, assumptions, user feedback, and theoretical
reflections. The process will ensure that both practical utility and
scientific contribution are achieved.

efficacy of the artifact following the Framework for Evaluation in

Both ex-ante (predictive) and ex-post (empirical) evaluations will be
planned.

-   System performance logs for technical metrics

-   Feedback surveys and interviews for qualitative insights

## **3.9 Data Analysis** 

Quantitative data will be analyzed using descriptive statistics (e.g.,
mean, standard deviation) and inferential methods (e.g., paired t-tests)
to assess improvement between system iterations. Qualitative feedback
will undergo thematic analysis, allowing identification of recurring
issues, user preferences, and emergent design principles.

Findings will guide further artifact refinement and serve as evidence
for the artifact's effectiveness.

**3.10 Ethical Considerations**

Ethical approval will be secured from the institution's ethics review
committee prior to data collection. Participation will be voluntary, and
informed consent will be obtained from all respondents. Data
confidentiality and anonymity will be strictly maintained, and all
information will be used solely for research purposes.
